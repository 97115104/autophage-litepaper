3Token Decay Dynamics

Definition 3.1. Token decay follows the exponential decay law $V(t) = V_0 e^{-\lambda t}$ where $\lambda = -\ln(1-\delta)$ and $\delta$ is the daily decay rate.

Each token species exhibits different decay characteristics aligned with its economic purpose:

  • Rhythm tokens ($\delta = 0.05$): High decay encourages daily activities
  • Healing tokens ($\delta = 0.0075$): Moderate decay for quarterly health cycles
  • Foundation tokens ($\delta = 0.001$): Low decay for long-term value storage
  • Catalyst tokens ($\delta \in [0.02, 0.10]$): Dynamic decay for system stabilization

5Biological Scaling Laws

Theorem 5.1 (Kleiber's Law for Networks). System efficiency scales as $\eta(N) = 1 + \alpha(N/N_0)^{1/4}$ where $N$ is network size, $\alpha = 0.3$, and $N_0 = 1000$.

The protocol incorporates biological scaling principles to ensure sustainable growth. As the network expands, efficiency increases sub-linearly, preventing runaway growth while rewarding scale. This mirrors metabolic efficiency in biological systems where larger organisms achieve better energy efficiency per unit mass.

6Conclusions

These simulations provide strong empirical support for the theoretical predictions of the Autophage Protocol. Key findings include:

  1. Wealth distribution consistently converges to target Gini coefficients of 0.32–0.38
  2. Token decay mechanisms effectively prevent wealth concentration while maintaining incentives
  3. System stability is maintained across wide ranges of network sizes and activity patterns
  4. Biological scaling laws successfully translate to economic contexts

These results suggest that incorporating biological principles into economic design can yield more equitable and sustainable systems. Future work will explore additional biological mechanisms and their potential economic applications.
